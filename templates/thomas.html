St. Thomas of Villanova

Augustinian friar, archbishop, and father of the poor (1488 to 1555).

Biography

Thomas was born in 1488 in Fuenllana, Spain, and raised in the town of Villanueva de los Infantes, from which he took his name. He studied and taught at the University of Alcalá, then entered the Augustinian Order at Salamanca in 1516. He became a respected preacher and teacher and later served as prior and provincial of his order.

In 1544 he was named Archbishop of Valencia, after first declining a similar appointment. He died on September 8, 1555, and was canonized in 1658. Villanova University in Pennsylvania takes its name from him.

Key values

  • Humility. He arrived in Valencia on foot, in a worn habit.
  • Charity. Daily alms and meals for the hungry were part of his routine.
  • Prayer and study. His sermons grew out of long reflection.
  • Simplicity. He kept his own life plain so others could have enough.

Service to the poor

Traditional accounts say that he gave his own bed to a beggar, that he fed hundreds at his door each day, and that he gave dowries to poor young women and helped orphans and the sick. He was called the "Father of the Poor." He is also remembered for helping the poor keep their dignity, not just their daily bread.

Legacy

His example still shapes Augustinian schools, parishes and charities around the world. His feast day is celebrated in September, and he remains a model of leadership that serves instead of rules.

Connection to this project

Thomas is the pelican of the board. His inner life, formed by Augustine's call to "return to yourself," led directly to self-giving. In an age that asks machines to think for us, he reminds us that the deepest human work, seeing another person's need and responding with love, cannot be automated.
