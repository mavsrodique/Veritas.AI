Room IV

The Symbols

Every symbol tells part of the story. Together they reveal the message of the exhibit.

Symbols can express in a single image what pages of text struggle to explain. Each symbol on this board connects St. Thomas of Villanova, Augustinian spirituality, and the questions raised by Artificial Intelligence.

The Pelican

Caritas. Charity. Service. Self-giving love.

Meaning. In Christian tradition, the pelican became a symbol of self-sacrificing love. Medieval Christians believed a pelican would feed its young with its own blood in times of need. Whether legend or reality, the image became a powerful symbol of charity and generosity.

Connection to St. Thomas. Known as the “Father of the Poor,” St. Thomas of Villanova devoted his life to serving those in need. The pelican represents the Caritas that defined his ministry and legacy.

The Mirror

Veritas. Reflection. Self-awareness. Truth.

Meaning. A mirror reflects what is truly present. It cannot flatter, exaggerate, or hide reality. It invites honesty and self-awareness.

Connection to Augustinian Interiority. St. Augustine taught that truth is found by turning inward. The mirror represents Veritas through reflection, self-examination, and conscience. In this exhibit, the final subject being studied is the viewer.

The Feathers

Reflection. Transformation. Growth. Self-discovery.

Meaning. Feathers symbolize growth, movement, and transformation. Like a person developing through reflection, they suggest a journey rather than a destination.

Purpose. The feathers on the physical bulletin board contain questions for personal reflection. They invite visitors to begin their own inward journey toward greater self-understanding.

Brain and Heart

Intelligence and Compassion. Knowledge and Wisdom.

Meaning. The brain represents knowledge, learning, logic, and innovation. The heart represents compassion, empathy, conscience, and love.

Connection. Artificial Intelligence may imitate reasoning, but it does not possess conscience, compassion, or moral responsibility. Human flourishing requires both intelligence and a heart. Knowledge tells us what can be done. Wisdom helps us decide what should be done.

The Gears

Human Innovation. Technology. Creativity.

Meaning. Gears represent engineering, invention, creativity, and the human ability to solve problems. Every machine begins with a human idea.

The Tension. Gears are precise and tireless, but they do not know why they turn. Beside the mirror, they invite us to ask what progress is for and who it should serve.

Artificial Intelligence

Innovation. Responsibility. Ethical Technology.

Meaning. Artificial Intelligence represents one of humanity's most powerful technological achievements. It can generate content, assist with research, recognize patterns, and support decision-making.

Connection. AI is a tool, not a substitute for the human person. The challenge of our time is not whether machines can become more human, but whether humans will continue to cultivate Veritas, Caritas, and responsible stewardship while using them.
