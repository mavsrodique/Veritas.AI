The Symbols

A guided tour of the board. Each piece is a clue. Scroll slowly and look at the board as you read.

The Pelican

Sacrifice. Charity. Generosity. Self-giving love.

Medieval tradition said a mother pelican would wound her own breast to feed her chicks with her blood. Christians adopted it as an image of Christ's self-giving love, and it appears in churches and on altars. It is a symbol of love that costs something.

Connection to St. Thomas. Thomas gave his bed, his food and his time to the poor until little was left for himself. The pelican is his portrait in symbol: an inner life so full of love that it overflows into giving.

The Mirror

Reflection. Self-examination. Self-awareness. Looking inward.

A mirror shows what is there, without flattery. On the board it is the place where the visitor sees their own face among the symbols. It is a pause in the middle of a display about technology.

Connection to Augustinian Interiority. Augustine invites us to look at ourselves as honestly as we look at the world. The mirror turns the exhibit around: the final piece being studied is you.

The Pullable Feathers

Reflection questions. Introspection. Growth. Self-awareness.

On the physical board, each feather hides a reflection question. Feathers are light and quiet, like the thoughts that arrive when we slow down. Take a moment at the board to try them in person.

Connection to your visit. You are not asked to read an answer. You are asked to answer. The Reflect page carries the same questions with you after you leave the board.

The Gears

Technology. Innovation. The digital age.

Gears are interlocking parts that turn together. They stand for engineering, progress and the systems that power modern life, from medicine to the phone in your hand. Their red color on the board marks the world of technology.

The tension. Gears are precise and tireless, but they do not know why they turn. The board places them beside the mirror to ask: what is all this progress for, and who decides?

Artificial Intelligence Elements

Human-AI interaction. Technology in modern life. Benefits and limits.

Benefits. AI can explain hard ideas, translate languages, help with research, assist doctors and open learning to more people.

Limits. It can be wrong while sounding sure. It has no conscience, no experience of suffering, and no responsibility for what it says. It reflects the data and the people behind it.

A healthy relationship. Treat AI as a tool that serves human dignity. Use it to gather information, then use your own judgment, conscience and heart to decide what to do. That is why Veritas AI on this site is optional and always points you back to your own reflection.
