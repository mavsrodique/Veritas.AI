Room VI

Artificial Intelligence

A powerful tool shaped by the knowledge, values, and choices of its makers.

Technology in modern life. Artificial Intelligence is already used in medicine, education, research, translation, accessibility, and many everyday digital services. It can expand access to information and assist people in tasks that once required far more time and resources.

Human and machine. People increasingly rely on AI for answers, explanations, and recommendations. Yet AI does not understand the world as people do. It predicts patterns from data and can sometimes produce biased, incomplete, or incorrect information. Human judgment remains essential.

Benefits and limits

What AI can do

Analyze large amounts of information quickly. Identify patterns. Generate text, images, and summaries. Assist learning and research. Support decision-making in many fields.

What only a person can do

Exercise conscience. Take moral responsibility. Form genuine relationships. Love, forgive, and sacrifice for others. Choose what is right even when it is difficult.

Ethical technology

Technology should serve the dignity of every human person. The value of an innovation is measured not only by what it can do, but also by how it affects people and communities.

Responsibility never belongs to a machine. The people who design, deploy, and use technology remain accountable for its consequences. Ethical use of AI requires honesty, fairness, transparency, and concern for the common good.

In the Augustinian tradition, truth must be joined with love. Veritas without Caritas can become cold and harmful; Caritas without Veritas can become misguided. Human wisdom requires both.
