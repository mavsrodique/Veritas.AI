The Project

A bulletin board about the oldest question: who am I, really?

Purpose

This board pairs a 16th-century Augustinian saint with a 21st-century technology. St. Thomas of Villanova lived a life of inner honesty that turned into generous action. Today we carry machines that can answer almost any question. The board asks what remains for us to do ourselves.

Why the theme matters

Saint Augustine taught that truth is found by turning inward, not only by looking outward. St. Thomas of Villanova, an Augustinian friar and archbishop, lived that teaching: he examined himself, then spent himself for the poor. His life shows that interiority is not escape from the world. It is where good action begins.

Why Artificial Intelligence is relevant today

AI now writes, summarizes, translates, recommends and decides alongside us, including in schools. It is powerful and useful. It also invites us to outsource thinking, and with it the habit of reflection. Students are the first generation to grow up beside it, so the question of how to use it well belongs to them.

Why interiority remains important

An AI can produce words about kindness. It cannot be grateful, repent, or choose to serve someone at a cost to itself. Conscience, gratitude, humility and love are formed in the person, in quiet. Technology can support that work. It cannot replace it.

The board's claim in one line: use the tool, but keep the inner life.
