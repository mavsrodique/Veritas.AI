Room I

The Project

A bulletin board about one of humanity's oldest questions: Who am I, really?

A saint of the sixteenth century. A technology of the twenty-first. One question held between them.

Then

St. Thomas of Villanova practiced Augustinian interiority. Through reflection, prayer, and self-examination, he sought truth within himself. What he discovered did not keep him turned inward. It moved him outward in service to the poor of Valencia.

Now

We live alongside machines that can answer questions, generate ideas, and provide information in seconds. They are powerful tools, but they can also make it easy to stop reflecting on ourselves.

The Purpose. This exhibit brings together an Augustinian saint and modern Artificial Intelligence to explore what remains uniquely human when knowledge and answers are always within reach.

Why the Theme Matters. St. Augustine taught that truth is discovered through interiority, the journey inward toward self-knowledge, wisdom, and God. St. Thomas of Villanova lived this teaching through a life of humility, charity, and service. Interiority is not an escape from the world. It is where meaningful action begins.

Why AI Is Relevant Today. Artificial Intelligence now assists with writing, research, translation, communication, and learning. For many students, it is becoming a daily companion. As the first generation to grow up alongside AI, young people face an important question: how can technology be used responsibly without losing reflection, critical thinking, and human connection?

Why Interiority Still Matters. Artificial Intelligence can produce words about kindness, wisdom, and compassion. It cannot possess conscience, humility, gratitude, or love. These qualities are formed within the human person through reflection, relationships, experience, and moral responsibility.

Use the tool. Keep the inner life.
