Augustinian Interiority

Do not go outside. Return to yourself. Truth lives in the inner person.

What interiority means

Interiority is the habit of turning your attention inward: to your thoughts, motives, desires and conscience. St. Augustine (354 to 430) wrote that God is "more inward than my most inward part." For him, the search for truth and the search for oneself were the same journey.

Self-examination

Ask honestly what you did today and why. Not to punish yourself, but to see clearly.

Reflection

Slow down long enough for a question to reach you. Quiet is where answers surface.

Looking within

Notice what you value, fear and love. These quietly steer your choices.

Personal growth

Seeing yourself truthfully makes change possible: a better habit, an apology, an act of service.

In simple words

Before you answer the world, answer yourself. What do I really think? What do I really want? Who am I becoming? Interiority is not about being alone forever. It is the quiet check-in that makes your actions honest.

Try it now: close your eyes for ten seconds and ask yourself, "What matters most to me today?"

Interiority and the digital age

Screens and AI answer fast. Inner work is slow. A machine can give you information about yourself, but only you can recognize what is true of you. The board holds both: the speed of technology and the stillness of the mirror.
